## Why this sheet exists

Corrosion damage on industrial steel structures is normally decided at the drawing stage, not at the maintenance stage. This sheet publishes the coating thickness ranges and component-by-component selection we apply on export projects, so any buyer can run the same check on any supplier's corrosion protection specification.

Two variables decide the outcome: **how corrosive the site actually is**, and **the shape of the detail** (drainage, water traps, site-cut edges, bolt holes).

---

## 1. Dry film thickness by system

| System | Environment | Dry film thickness |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-coat paint (zinc-rich primer + intermediate + UV/abrasion topcoat) | Mildly to moderately corrosive inland site | 160–240 µm (6.3–9.4 mils) across the complete system |
| Multi-coat paint (high-build, zinc-rich primer + epoxy + polyurethane topcoat) | Highly corrosive coastal site | 200–280 µm (7.9–11.0 mils) across the complete system |
| Hot-dip galvanizing (HDG) | General structural sections | 45–85 µm (1.8–3.3 mils) zinc coating |

HDG is thinner than a paint system but protects by a different mechanism: the zinc is metallurgically bonded, and where the coating is damaged the surrounding zinc corrodes preferentially to protect the exposed steel.

## 2. Protection by building component

| Component | Recommended protection | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Primary structural members (columns, rafters) | High-build paint — zinc-rich primer + epoxy + polyurethane topcoat, near-white blast preparation | Large members, handled carefully, protected by cladding |
| Secondary purlins and girts | Hot-dip galvanized | High surface area, site-cut frequently, abrasion-prone during installation |
| Bolts, nuts, washers | Hot-dip galvanized, or mechanical zinc plating | Small parts, high surface-to-volume ratio, critical fastening function |
| Anchor bolts (below-grade or grout-encased) | Hot-dip galvanized | Partially inaccessible after construction; repair extremely difficult |
| Roof and wall cladding | Aluminium-zinc alloy coated steel, or HDG with colour coat | Thin gauge — paint-only on thin sheet has a limited life in highly corrosive coastal exposure |
| Crane beams and runway rails | Heavy-build epoxy paint, with a defined inspection protocol | Hot-dip galvanizing is not typically available in the required sections |

## 3. Service-life expectations

| Item | Expectation |
|---|---|
| HDG, maintenance-free interval | 20–40 years in most environments (governed by site corrosivity) |
| Good-quality three-coat paint system, inland site | 15–20 years, with annual inspection and minor spot repairs |
| Coastal zone designation (tropical climate) | Within roughly 1–3 km (0.6–1.9 miles) of open sea — severely corrosive |
| Relative degradation, most aggressive coastal exposure vs moderately corrosive inland | Approximately double the rate for conventional paint systems |

---

## 4. Field checks we ask for

1. **Surface preparation** — near-white blast cleaning verified by visual comparison against reference photographs of correctly prepared surfaces, or by a surface profile gauge. Power-tool grinding without blasting shortens service life regardless of paint quality.
2. **Dry film thickness** — measured per coat and at final completion with a certified gauge. The specification must state minimum and maximum thickness for each coat and for the total system.
